# Scene Patterns and Transition Recipes

These are compositional patterns, not rigid templates. Choose only what supports the message and reference.

## 1. Minimal hook

**Purpose:** Make one promise or create curiosity.

- Start with a calm frame or a purposeful first motion.
- Reveal a short headline with intentional line breaks.
- Use one accent element to guide the eye.
- Hold long enough to read.
- Transition using the headline, a shape, or a camera move that motivates the next beat.

Avoid: several headlines competing, random particles, or a loud transition before the idea is understood.

## 2. Problem to solution

**Purpose:** Show friction and then relief.

- Present the problem in a visually constrained or fragmented composition.
- Use a single transition event to clear or reorganize the frame.
- Reveal the solution with more space, alignment, and calm.
- Make the visual contrast communicate the benefit without relying on unsupported superlatives.

## 3. Product / UI reveal

**Purpose:** Make a product mechanism understandable.

- Establish a clear hero plane or device/card.
- Introduce the main UI before secondary details.
- Use depth and stagger to direct attention.
- Keep UI text plausible and legible.
- Resolve into a stable, readable product view.

Avoid rotating every card independently or using tiny fake copy as visual noise.

## 4. Feature sequence

**Purpose:** Communicate several benefits without feeling like slides.

- Give each feature a distinct visual beat.
- Reuse a shared grid, typography, and transition grammar.
- Let the current feature transform, move, or hand off focus to the next.
- Vary intensity; not every beat needs a camera move.
- Remove any feature that does not strengthen the core story.

## 5. Before / after

**Purpose:** Make a transformation clear.

- Keep comparison conditions consistent.
- Use clear labels.
- Transition on a meaningful change in composition or behavior.
- Avoid manipulating the comparison in a misleading way.
- Keep the improved state long enough to inspect.

## 6. Typography as transition

**Purpose:** Turn a key word into the next scene's object.

- Choose a word with a meaningful shape or semantic relationship to the next visual.
- Animate position/scale with a controlled curve.
- Match its exit geometry to the incoming object.
- Maintain readability until the transition intentionally begins.
- Ensure the transform feels designed, not like a random morph.

## 7. Floating object / cards

**Purpose:** Create depth around a product.

- Establish a stable hero anchor.
- Use small differences in depth, scale, and timing.
- Keep movement low-amplitude and intentional.
- Use consistent lighting and shadows.
- Let secondary objects settle before the hero message changes.

Avoid perpetual bobbing, arbitrary orbit paths, and excessive blur.

## 8. CTA end card

**Purpose:** Convert attention into a clear next step.

- Reduce visual complexity.
- State one action with exact copy.
- Keep brand/product identity clear.
- Hold the CTA long enough to read.
- Finish animation and audio cleanly; avoid cutting mid-motion.

## 9. Transition bridge checklist

Before adding any transition, answer:
- What connects the two scenes?
- What does the viewer track through the transition?
- Where does attention land?
- Is the transition faster than comprehension?
- Would a clean cut be stronger?

If no meaningful answer exists, use a clean cut or redesign the scene connection.
